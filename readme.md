# Computational Investigation of TYK2 as a Therapeutic Target in Ankylosing Spondylitis

A reproducible analysis of whether TYK2 is a credible drug target in ankylosing spondylitis (AS),
and whether public chemistry contains compounds that could start a programme against it.

Everything here is built from public data (ChEMBL 37, Open Targets) and regenerated by one command.

## Headline findings

**1. The target rationale for AS is mechanistic, not genetic.**
TYK2 ranks 10th of 1,743 targets associated with AS in Open Targets, but that rank rests almost
entirely on *clinical precedent* — pan-JAK drugs (upadacitinib, tofacitinib, filgotinib) that inhibit
TYK2 among other kinases and are used in AS. The human genetic evidence for TYK2 in AS is weak
(genetic association score 0.09), and the two GWAS credible sets overlapping TYK2 assign their signal
to neighbouring genes (PDE4A, RAVER1) at least as strongly. By contrast TYK2 genetics are strong in
psoriasis (0.94), rheumatoid arthritis (0.94), SLE (0.88) and Crohn disease (0.80). The honest
position: **well-motivated through the IL-23/IL-17 axis and supported by precedent in related
spondyloarthropathies, but not genetically validated in AS itself.**

**2. Selectivity over JAK1/2/3 is the hard part, and it is rare.**
Of 5,913 TYK2 compounds, 3,096 have measured data against at least one other JAK — and only 21 clear
100-fold selectivity over every JAK they were tested against on a like-for-like basis. The median TYK2
compound is *not* TYK2-selective. This is why deucravacitinib's JH2 (pseudokinase) allosteric
mechanism mattered: the ATP site is too conserved across the family to give selectivity easily.

**3. 37% of the TYK2 potency data is binned, not measured.**
Fifteen documents (patent extractions) report potency as ranges stored as single values — 0.3, 0.6,
5.5, 55 nM repeated hundreds of times. They account for 80% of the JH2 compounds. Naively differencing
these values produces selectivity of exactly zero for hundreds of compounds. The pipeline flags them
and excludes them from regression and from all selectivity arithmetic.

**4. The prioritisation recovers the drug that already works.**
17 compounds pass every filter, across 15 scaffolds. Deucravacitinib — the only approved
TYK2-selective inhibitor — passes and ranks 7th, with no special handling; all 12 other clinical
JAK-family drugs in the dataset fail on selectivity. That the method keeps the selective drug and
rejects the pan-JAK ones is the strongest available check that the filters encode the right profile.

**5. QSAR models work within known series, not beyond them.**
A Morgan-fingerprint random forest predicts pChEMBL at RMSE 0.73 under a scaffold split (0.65 random),
against experimental noise of ~0.66; classification of actives reaches ROC AUC 0.93. Error rises
sharply beyond Tanimoto 0.5 from the training set, so the model ranks analogues — it does not discover
chemotypes.

## Quick start

```bash
python -m venv .venv && .venv/Scripts/activate   # Windows; use bin/activate on macOS/Linux
pip install -r requirements.txt
python run_pipeline.py
```

`run_pipeline.py --skip-download` reuses whatever is already in `data/raw/`.
`run_pipeline.py --notebooks` also re-executes the notebooks. A full run takes roughly 30–40 minutes,
most of it downloading ChEMBL.

## Layout

```
src/
  get_chembl_data.py     download TYK2 + JAK1/2/3 activities from ChEMBL      -> data/raw/
  target_evidence.py     TYK2<->AS evidence, GWAS loci, drugs from Open Targets -> data/raw/opentargets/
  process_activities.py  clean, standardise, flag binned data, descriptors    -> data/processed/
  selectivity.py         TYK2 vs JAK1/2/3 selectivity windows                 -> data/processed/
  model.py               QSAR regression + classification, scaffold splits    -> results/

  02_exploratory_analysis.ipynb            dataset characterisation and data quality
  03_target_evidence_and_candidates.ipynb  target evidence, selectivity, models, candidates
tests/                   unit tests for the data-processing logic
figures/                 all generated figures
results/                 model metrics, CV predictions, candidate rankings
```

## Method notes

**Standardisation.** Salts and solvents stripped, charges neutralised, canonical SMILES; repeated
measurements collapsed to a median per structure. Compounds are keyed on standardised structure, not
ChEMBL ID, so salt forms of the same molecule merge.

**Binned-value detection.** A document is flagged when it has ≥20 records and either ≤5 distinct
values or ≥90% of records falling on its 5 most common values. Compounds get both `pchembl_median`
(everything) and `pchembl_measured` (non-binned only). The classification task can use binned data
because the 100 nM threshold sits between bin values; regression and selectivity cannot.

**Selectivity caveat.** TYK2 JH2 binding constants are not directly comparable with JAK1/2/3 catalytic
IC50s — different site, different assay format. Those comparisons are computed but flagged
`cross_assay`, and the like-for-like count is reported separately.

**Validation splits.** 91% of compounds have a neighbour at Tanimoto ≥ 0.7, so random splits leak
close analogues. Every model is reported under both random and scaffold-grouped 5-fold CV; the
scaffold number is the one to believe.

**Candidate filters.** pChEMBL ≥ 8 (≤ 10 nM); ≥ 100-fold selectivity over every JAK tested, with at
least one tested; ≤ 1 Lipinski violation; TPSA ≤ 140; no PAINS alerts. Survivors are scored on
potency, selectivity, completeness of the off-target profile, ligand efficiency and QED, then capped
at two compounds per Bemis-Murcko scaffold.

## Limitations

- Public data only: ChEMBL is biased toward published and patented chemistry, and the most advanced
  compounds are often the least completely reported.
- Most shortlisted compounds were profiled against only one or two JAKs. Scoring penalises this but
  cannot substitute for a full panel.
- No structural work — no docking or free-energy calculation. Selectivity here is purely empirical.
- Assay conditions (ATP concentration in particular) vary across sources and are not normalised.
- Open Targets scores are aggregates of heterogeneous evidence and are used as published.

## Data sources

- **ChEMBL 37** (2026-05-01) — bioactivity data, via the EBI REST API. TYK2 `CHEMBL3553`,
  JAK1 `CHEMBL2835`, JAK2 `CHEMBL2971`, JAK3 `CHEMBL2148`.
- **Open Targets Platform** — target-disease associations and evidence, via the GraphQL API.
  Ankylosing spondylitis `MONDO_0005306`, TYK2 `ENSG00000105397`.

Raw downloads are gitignored; rerun the pipeline to recreate them.
